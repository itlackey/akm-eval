# A Counteroffer Terminates the Original Offer
Under the mirror-image rule, a reply that changes any term of an offer —
price, quantity, delivery date, warranty language — is treated as a
counteroffer, not an acceptance. The legal effect of a counteroffer is to
terminate the original offer outright; the original offeror is no longer
bound by it and the original offeree cannot later go back and simply
"accept" the original terms as if nothing happened. Clients frequently
assume they can negotiate freely and then fall back to the first deal if
negotiations stall, but once they have proposed different terms, that
option is gone unless the other side chooses to revive it. When advising on
a negotiation, flag this explicitly before a client sends a counter-proposal
they might later want to retract in favor of the original terms.
