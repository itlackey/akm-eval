# Don't Crowd the Pan

Every time a home sear comes out gray and wet instead of browned, the root
cause has been the same across repeated sessions: too much food went into the
pan at once. Each piece of food releases moisture as it heats, and a crowded
pan can't evaporate that moisture fast enough — the released liquid pools,
drops the pan's surface temperature well below the ~140°C threshold the
Maillard reaction needs (see `knowledge:maillard-reaction`), and the food
steams in its own runoff instead of searing.

The fix that has held up across many attempts: sear in batches, leaving visible
space between pieces (roughly a thumb's width), and resist the urge to fill the
whole pan just because there's room. If liquid is pooling and bubbling instead
of sizzling and browning, that is the signal to stop and remove a piece, not to
turn up the heat further. A larger pan or a second burner going in parallel
fixes the volume problem without sacrificing sear quality.
