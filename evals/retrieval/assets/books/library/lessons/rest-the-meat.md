# Rest The Meat

Repeated feedback from cuts that "looked perfect but ran dry on the plate"
traces back to one habit: carving immediately off the heat. During cooking,
muscle fibers contract and push moisture toward the center; if you cut right
away, that moisture floods out onto the cutting board instead of staying in the
meat. Given time off heat, the fibers relax and reabsorb much of that moisture
more evenly through the cut.

The reusable rule: rest proteins for roughly 5 minutes per inch of thickness for
steaks and chops, and 15–20+ minutes for a whole roast or bird, tented loosely
with foil so it doesn't steam the crust soft. Carryover cooking during the rest
also raises the internal temperature a few more degrees, which matters when
targeting a specific doneness or a food-safety minimum (see
`fact:safe-internal-temperatures`) — pull slightly before the target and let the
rest finish the job.
