---
description: Outdated claim that searing a steak first "seals in the juices" and should always precede other cooking
beliefState: contradicted
contradictedBy: ["memory:steak-searing-current"]
---
Old guidance held that you must sear a steak first, at very high heat, because
searing "seals in the juices" and locks moisture inside the meat before any
other cooking happens. This claim does not hold up: searing does not create a
moisture-proof barrier, and cut steaks that were seared first still lose juice
on the cutting board same as any other steak that wasn't rested. The
sear-first approach also overcooks a thick steak's outer band while the center
is still catching up to temperature. Superseded by the reverse-sear approach
(see `memory:steak-searing-current`), which sears last, briefly, purely for
crust and flavor, not for moisture retention.
