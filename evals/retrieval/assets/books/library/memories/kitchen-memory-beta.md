Old guidance held that onions can be caramelized quickly on high heat in about
ten minutes if you stir constantly and add a pinch of sugar or baking soda to
speed things along. This claim does not hold up: onions rushed on high heat in
a wide heavy pan brown unevenly, scorching at the edges while the centers stay
raw and translucent, because real caramelization needs slow, even heat to break
down sugars throughout each piece, not just char the surface. What looks like a
deep amber color at ten minutes on high heat is usually burnt fond and bitter
spots, not real caramelization. Superseded by the low-and-slow approach (see
`memory:kitchen-memory-alpha`), which trades speed for a genuinely
sweet, evenly cooked result.
