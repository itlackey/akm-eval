---
description: The current best-practice method for searing a thick steak — reverse sear for even doneness plus a hard crust
---
Current best method for a thick steak (1.5 inches or more): reverse sear. Season
and let the steak air-dry uncovered in the fridge for an hour or more, then cook
low and slow in a 250°F oven (or on the cool side of a grill) until it reaches
roughly 10–15°F below the target internal temperature, then finish with a very
hard, hot sear in a screaming-hot pan or over direct high heat for 60–90 seconds
per side to build the crust. This produces edge-to-edge even doneness with a
deep crust, unlike searing from raw which overcooks a wide outer band before the
center catches up. Rest before slicing (see `lesson:rest-the-meat`) and avoid
crowding the pan during the final sear (see `lesson:dont-crowd-the-pan`).
