# Statute of Limitations Overview
A statute of limitations is a law that sets the maximum time after an event
within which legal proceedings may be initiated. Once the limitations period
expires, a claim is generally time-barred and a court will dismiss it
regardless of its underlying merits, even if the defendant is clearly at
fault. Limitations periods vary widely by claim type and jurisdiction: many
personal injury claims allow two to three years, written contract claims
often allow four to six years, and some crimes such as murder have no
limitations period at all. The clock typically starts running when the
cause of action "accrues" — usually when the harm occurs or is discovered —
and can be paused ("tolled") in specific circumstances, such as when the
plaintiff is a minor, the defendant is absent from the jurisdiction, or fraud
concealed the injury. The policy rationale is to encourage timely
resolution of disputes while evidence and witness memories are still fresh,
and to give potential defendants eventual repose from stale claims.
