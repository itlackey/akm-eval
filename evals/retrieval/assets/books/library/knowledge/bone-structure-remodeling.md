# Bone Structure and Remodeling

Bone is a living, dynamic connective tissue, not an inert scaffold. It is
continuously broken down and rebuilt throughout life.

## Structural types

- **Compact (cortical) bone** — dense, forms the outer shell of all bones and
  the shafts of long bones; organized into osteons (Haversian systems), each
  a set of concentric lamellae around a central canal carrying blood vessels
  and nerves.
- **Spongy (cancellous/trabecular) bone** — porous, found at the ends of long
  bones and inside flat bones; the trabecular lattice houses red bone marrow
  and is lighter while still resisting the forces typical at joints.

## Bone cells

- **Osteoblasts** — build new bone matrix (osteoid) and mineralize it.
- **Osteocytes** — mature osteoblasts trapped in the matrix; they maintain
  the tissue and sense mechanical strain.
- **Osteoclasts** — large multinucleated cells that resorb bone matrix,
  releasing calcium and phosphate into the blood.

## Remodeling cycle

Remodeling couples resorption and formation at the same site: osteoclasts
first resorb a small volume of old or damaged bone, then osteoblasts fill
the cavity with new matrix. This cycle repairs microdamage, adapts bone
architecture to mechanical loading (Wolff's law), and contributes to
calcium homeostasis — when blood calcium is low, parathyroid hormone
stimulates osteoclast activity to release stored calcium.
