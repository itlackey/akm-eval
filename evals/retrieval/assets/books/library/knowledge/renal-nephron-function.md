# Nephron Function

The nephron is the functional unit of the kidney. Roughly one million
nephrons per kidney carry out three processes that together produce urine
and regulate blood composition.

## The three processes

1. **Glomerular filtration** — blood pressure forces water and small solutes
   from glomerular capillaries into Bowman's capsule, forming filtrate.
   Blood cells and large proteins normally stay in the capillary because the
   filtration membrane excludes them by size and charge.
2. **Tubular reabsorption** — as filtrate moves through the proximal
   convoluted tubule, loop of Henle, distal convoluted tubule, and collecting
   duct, useful substances (glucose, amino acids, most water and
   electrolytes) are reclaimed back into peritubular capillary blood.
3. **Tubular secretion** — the tubule cells actively move additional
   substances (hydrogen ions, potassium, some drugs and toxins) from blood
   into the filtrate, supplementing filtration for waste removal.

## Regional specialization

- **Proximal convoluted tubule** — bulk reabsorption (~65% of filtrate),
  glucose and amino acid reabsorption via cotransporters.
- **Loop of Henle** — establishes the medullary concentration gradient via
  countercurrent multiplication, enabling urine concentration.
- **Distal convoluted tubule and collecting duct** — fine-tuned, hormonally
  regulated reabsorption of sodium and water (aldosterone, ADH).

## Glomerular filtration rate (GFR)

GFR is the volume of filtrate formed per minute across all nephrons and is
the standard measure of overall kidney function; it is regulated by
afferent/efferent arteriolar tone and autoregulated across a range of
systemic blood pressures.
