---
description: Mailbox rule note. Acceptance is effective at dispatch versus receipt
searchHints: ["Before advising a client that a mailed reply only counts once read on the other end"]
---
Under the mailbox rule, once an offeree properly dispatches an acceptance
by an authorized means, the acceptance is effective the moment it is sent,
not when the offeror actually receives it. A contract can form even if the
acceptance is delayed, lost, or received after a revocation that the
offeror mailed but the offeree never saw. Many people assume an acceptance
only counts once the other side reads it, but the rule cuts the other way:
what matters is the moment of dispatch, not receipt. This rule gives
offerees certainty once they have done everything within their control to
accept, shifting the risk of transmission failure onto the offeror
instead.
