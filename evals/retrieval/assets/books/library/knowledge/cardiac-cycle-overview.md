# The Cardiac Cycle

The cardiac cycle is the sequence of mechanical and electrical events that
repeats with every heartbeat. It is divided into two main phases: **systole**
(ventricular contraction and ejection) and **diastole** (ventricular
relaxation and filling).

## Phases

1. **Atrial systole** — the atria contract, pushing the last ~20% of blood
   into the ventricles (the "atrial kick").
2. **Isovolumetric ventricular contraction** — the ventricles begin to
   contract with all valves closed, so pressure rises but volume does not
   change. This is when the first heart sound (S1) occurs, produced by
   closure of the mitral and tricuspid (atrioventricular, AV) valves.
3. **Ventricular ejection** — once ventricular pressure exceeds aortic and
   pulmonary artery pressure, the semilunar valves (aortic and pulmonary)
   open and blood is ejected.
4. **Isovolumetric ventricular relaxation** — the semilunar valves close
   (producing the second heart sound, S2) and the ventricles relax with all
   valves shut.
5. **Ventricular filling** — the AV valves open and blood flows passively
   from the atria into the relaxed ventricles, completing the cycle.

## Why it matters clinically

Auscultation timing (S1 vs S2), pulse pressure, and jugular venous pulsations
are all interpreted relative to these phases. Murmurs are named for the phase
in which they occur (systolic vs diastolic), which narrows the differential
for valve pathology.

This is general physiology education content, not diagnostic guidance for any
specific patient.
