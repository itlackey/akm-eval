# Tolling and Repose Exceptions
This explainer works through a statute of limitations overview from a
different angle: the exceptions. A statute of limitations overview usually
starts with the basic filing deadline, but a full statute of limitations
overview also has to cover tolling — the pausing of the clock during
minority, incapacity, or a defendant's absence from the jurisdiction.
Fraudulent concealment is another common tolling ground worth flagging in
any statute of limitations overview, since a defendant's active concealment
of the injury can pause the clock until discovery. A statute of limitations
overview aimed at practitioners should also flag statutes of repose, which
cut off a claim after a fixed period regardless of when the injury is
discovered — unlike an ordinary statute of limitations overview, a repose
period is not tolled by discovery rules at all. Keep this statute of
limitations overview handy whenever a claim looks time-barred on its face
but a tolling exception might still save it.
