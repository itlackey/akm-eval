# The Five Mother Sauces

Classical French cuisine (Escoffier's system) organizes sauces into five "mother"
sauces, each built from a distinct base and thickening method:

1. **Béchamel** — milk thickened with a white roux. Base for Mornay (add cheese)
   and cream sauces.
2. **Velouté** — light stock (chicken, veal, or fish) thickened with a blond roux.
   Base for suprême and allemande sauces.
3. **Espagnole** — brown stock thickened with a brown roux and tomato paste. Base
   for demi-glace and bordelaise.
4. **Sauce Tomate** — tomatoes simmered down, sometimes with a light roux or just
   reduction. Base for countless Italian- and Mediterranean-leaning sauces.
5. **Hollandaise** — emulsified egg yolk and clarified butter, acidulated with
   lemon juice, thickened by emulsion rather than starch. Base for béarnaise
   (add tarragon and shallot reduction) and choron (add tomato).

Learning the five lets you improvise: once you know a béchamel is milk + roux,
any dairy-based sauce becomes a variation rather than a new recipe to memorize.
