# Stock and Broth Basics

A good stock is the base of soups, sauces, and braises. The core method:

1. **Bones (for stock) or meat (for broth)** — roast bones first for a brown
   stock with deeper flavor, or use raw bones/meat for a lighter white stock.
2. **Mirepoix** — roughly 2 parts onion, 1 part carrot, 1 part celery by weight,
   added partway through so the vegetables don't overcook into mush.
3. **Cold water start** — starting with cold water and bringing it up slowly
   helps proteins release cleanly, producing a clearer stock.
4. **Gentle simmer, never a boil** — a rolling boil emulsifies fat into the
   liquid and makes the stock cloudy; keep it at a bare simmer with only
   occasional bubbles.
5. **Skim the scum** — impurities that rise as foam in the first 20–30 minutes
   should be skimmed off for a clean-tasting result.
6. **Simmer time** — roughly 4–6 hours for chicken, 8–12+ hours for beef or veal
   bones (or use a pressure cooker to compress this), then strain through a fine
   mesh or cheesecloth.

Cool stock quickly (ice bath, shallow containers) before refrigerating to keep
it food-safe.
