# Fiscal Policy and Budget Deficits
Fiscal policy is the use of government spending and taxation to influence
aggregate demand and economic activity. Expansionary fiscal policy —
increased spending or tax cuts — is typically used to stimulate a weak
economy, while contractionary fiscal policy — reduced spending or tax
increases — is used to cool an overheating one or to rein in debt. A budget
deficit occurs when government spending in a given year exceeds tax revenue
collected that year; deficits are financed by issuing government bonds,
which accumulate as public debt over time. Persistent deficits can crowd out
private investment by competing for loanable funds and raising interest
rates, though the size of this effect is debated among economists,
particularly when the economy is operating below full employment. Automatic
stabilizers — programs like unemployment insurance and progressive income
tax brackets — reduce the deficit during expansions and widen it during
recessions without any new legislation, smoothing the business cycle.
