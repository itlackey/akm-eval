# Standard Deduction Basics
The standard deduction is a fixed dollar amount that individual taxpayers can
subtract from adjusted gross income before computing federal income tax owed,
as an alternative to itemizing deductions such as mortgage interest and
charitable gifts. Filers choose whichever amount is larger: the flat standard
deduction or the sum of their allowable itemized deductions. The amount
depends on filing status (single, married filing jointly, married filing
separately, head of household) and is adjusted upward each year for
inflation. Taxpayers who are blind or age 65 or older receive an additional
increment on top of the base amount. Because roughly nine in ten filers now
take the standard deduction rather than itemizing, understanding the current
threshold is central to basic individual tax planning.
