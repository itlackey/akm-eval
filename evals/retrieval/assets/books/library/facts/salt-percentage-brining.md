# Salt Percentage For Brining

Standing convention for a wet brine: **5–6% salt by weight of the total brine
liquid** (water plus any other liquid), for a soak of roughly 8–12 hours for a
whole chicken or turkey, shorter (1–4 hours) for smaller cuts like chops.

Guidelines:

- Weigh water and salt rather than using volume measures — salt density varies
  enough between brands (table, kosher, flaked) that volume measures are
  unreliable.
- Above roughly 6% the meat can start to taste noticeably salty and take on a
  spongier texture; below roughly 3% the brine is mostly flavoring rather than
  doing meaningful moisture retention.
- Dry brining (salting directly and resting uncovered in the fridge) uses a much
  smaller amount, roughly 0.5–1% of the meat's weight in salt, because there is
  no dilution from a soaking liquid.
- Always brine in the refrigerator, and rinse and pat dry before cooking so the
  surface can still brown (a wet surface fights the Maillard reaction — see
  `knowledge:maillard-reaction`).
