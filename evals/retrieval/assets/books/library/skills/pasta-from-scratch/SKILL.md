# Pasta From Scratch

Guides mixing, kneading, resting, rolling, and cutting fresh pasta dough.

## When to use

Reach for this skill whenever the task is "make fresh pasta" — tagliatelle,
fettuccine, ravioli, or lasagna sheets — rather than cooking dried, store-bought
pasta.

## Steps

1. **Mix**: mound flour, well in the center, crack in eggs, and whisk the eggs
   with a fork while slowly incorporating flour from the inner wall.
2. **Knead**: once it comes together, knead by hand 8–10 minutes until smooth
   and elastic. See `knowledge:pasta-dough-ratios` for exact flour-to-egg
   ratios.
3. **Rest**: wrap tightly (plastic or a bowl) and rest at room temperature at
   least 30 minutes, up to a few hours refrigerated, so gluten relaxes.
4. **Roll**: divide into manageable pieces, flatten, and pass through a pasta
   roller (or rolling pin) from the widest setting down, folding occasionally
   on the first couple of passes to even the dough.
5. **Cut**: dust lightly with flour or semolina to prevent sticking, then cut
   into the target shape — wide ribbons for pappardelle, narrow for tagliatelle,
   or leave sheets whole for lasagna/ravioli.
6. **Cook fresh**: fresh pasta cooks in 1–3 minutes in well-salted boiling
   water — much faster than dried pasta, and it floats when done.

## Common failure modes

- Dough that won't stop springing back was not rested long enough.
- Dough that tears while rolling is too dry — add a few drops of water.
- Sticky, gummy noodles usually mean insufficient dusting flour before cutting.
