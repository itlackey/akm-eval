# First Aid Response

A general-education framework for responding to a first aid situation. This
is not a substitute for certified first aid/CPR training or emergency
medical services.

## Sequence

1. **Check scene safety** before approaching. Do not become a second casualty
   (traffic, electrical hazards, structural collapse, hazardous materials).
2. **Check responsiveness** — tap and shout. If unresponsive, call for
   emergency medical help (or delegate that call to a bystander) before
   continuing.
3. **Airway** — verify the airway is open and unobstructed before assessing
   breathing.
4. **Breathing** — look, listen, and feel for normal breathing for no more
   than 10 seconds. Absent or agonal (gasping) breathing means begin CPR.
5. **Circulation** — control any severe external bleeding with direct
   pressure before addressing lesser injuries.

## When to escalate immediately

- No normal breathing, or only gasping.
- Uncontrolled bleeding that soaks through direct pressure.
- Suspected spinal injury — minimize movement, stabilize in place.
- Signs of stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty) — note
  the time symptoms started; this determines treatment options.

## Guiding principle

Do the highest-impact, lowest-risk action first (scene safety, calling for
help, airway) before addressing visually dramatic but lower-priority injuries.
