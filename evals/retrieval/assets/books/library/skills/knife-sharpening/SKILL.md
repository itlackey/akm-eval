# Knife Sharpening

Guides sharpening a dull kitchen knife edge and maintaining it between sharpenings.

## When to use

Reach for this skill when a knife is dragging through food instead of slicing,
or as part of routine kitchen-equipment maintenance.

## Sharpening vs. honing

- **Sharpening** removes metal to create a new edge (whetstone, pull-through
  sharpener, or professional service). Needed every few months for a
  home cook, more often for heavy daily use.
- **Honing** realigns an existing edge that has rolled slightly out of true; it
  removes no metal. Use a honing steel before or after most cooking sessions —
  it does not replace sharpening.

## Whetstone steps

1. Soak a water stone (if required by the stone) for 10–15 minutes.
2. Hold the blade at a consistent angle, typically 15–20 degrees for Western
   knives, 10–15 for Japanese single-bevel knives.
3. Draw the blade across the stone in smooth strokes, alternating sides, working
   from coarse grit to fine grit.
4. Check for a burr (a thin curl of metal along the far edge) to confirm you've
   reached the whole edge before moving to the next grit.
5. Strop on the finest stone or a leather strop to remove the burr and finish.

## Maintenance

- Hand wash and dry knives immediately; never run them through a dishwasher.
- Store in a knife block, on a magnetic strip, or with blade guards — a loose
  drawer dulls and chips edges (see `memory:knife-storage-best-practice`).
- Use a wood or plastic cutting board; glass and stone boards dull edges fast.
